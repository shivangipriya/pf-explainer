PF Rejection Explainer

A Provident Fund claim can be rejected for many reasons - some are about records and documents, others about eligibility. This page explains several rejection reasons that official sources describe, notes recent rule changes, and keeps a private record of your claim and every step you take. The exact remark on your claim and the rules that apply to it determine the next step. This page cannot check your claim, contact EPFO, or file anything for you.

Read this first. Rejection reasons vary. Some involve records or documents; others involve eligibility, and those are not fixed by paperwork alone. Correcting a detail and resubmitting is a possible step, never a guarantee of approval. The reasons below are some of those described in official sources - not a complete list - and this page cannot see your claim or predict its outcome.

The common rejection reasons, in plain words

Bank account or KYC not seeded, or not approved

Your PF account pays out only to a bank account that EPFO has on record and verified against your UAN. If the bank detail is missing, typed wrong, or never verified, the claim stops here.

What to do: check the bank detail on the UAN member portal. Since April 2025, adding or changing your bank account no longer needs your employer's approval in the usual case - you can seed it yourself on the portal. EPFO has also removed the cancelled-cheque-leaf upload that used to cause many rejections.

Source: PIB release, 16 Jun 2025 (EPFO reform measures), pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2136592

Your name, date of birth or father's name does not match Aadhaar

EPFO checks your PF record against Aadhaar. Even a small difference - an initial expanded on one side, a different spelling - can hold or reject the claim because the system cannot confirm both records belong to you.

What to do: correct the PF record to match Aadhaar. Online profile correction through Aadhaar authentication is now available, and in most cases no longer depends on your employer or an EPFO office approving it.

Source: PIB release, 16 Jun 2025

Employer approval demanded for a transfer claim

Older transfer claims often waited on the employer to approve them. That requirement has been removed for most transfer claims with effect from 15 January 2025.

What to do: if a transfer claim is pending or was rejected for want of employer approval after that date, resubmit it on the portal - and if it is rejected again on the same ground, raise it on EPFiGMS (see Next steps).

Source: PIB release, 16 Jun 2025

Wrong or outdated form, or missing attestation on a paper claim

The online route uses a single composite claim form. The older Form 19 instructions exist for the paper route: a paper form goes through your last employer, or - when that is not possible - must be attested by one of the authorised officials listed in those instructions (for example a Magistrate, Gazetted Officer or Post Master). An unattested paper form is rejected. These attestation and SMS details describe the offline route; the online claim follows the portal's own steps.

What to do: prefer the online route on the UAN member portal. If you must file on paper, follow the attestation instructions on the form exactly.

Sources: Form 19 general instructions (epfindia.gov.in); EPFO Members FAQ (undated - treat details as needing confirmation on the portal)

Eligibility rules: minimum balance and waiting period

Some rejections are about eligibility, not paperwork. Withdrawal eligibility rules have changed recently and may change again: a provision required members to keep 25% of contributions as a minimum balance and to wait 12 months for final withdrawal; on 19 Nov 2026 the Central Board of Trustees approved reducing the minimum balance to 10% and the waiting period to 2 months. This is a Board approval, not yet a rule in force: the PIB release says the necessary amendments to the EPF Scheme will be notified after Ministry approval, and it gives no effective date. As of 25 Nov 2026 no notification was found. The Government's own descriptions treat 25% and 12 months as the existing provisions (PIB 19 Nov 2026 calls 25% "existing"; a Lok Sabha written reply of 10 Aug 2026 says EPFO has introduced the 12-month wait), so those remain the ones to plan around until a notification changes them.

What to do: check the current rules on the EPFO portal before resubmitting. If your claim was rejected under rules that have since changed, ask on EPFiGMS how the change affects your case.

Source: PIB release, 19 Nov 2026 (240th CBT meeting), pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2338667; Lok Sabha unstarred question 3567, 10 Aug 2026, sansad.in

Something this page does not recognise

Rejection remarks vary by office and change over time. If your remark does not match anything above, this page will not guess.

What to do: copy the exact remark into your claim record on the My claim tab, then raise it on EPFiGMS with the remark quoted word for word. Asking EPFO directly is the reliable move when the remark is unfamiliar.

What this page deliberately does not say

  • It is not a complete list of rejection reasons. Offices and claim types vary; the reasons here are the ones current official material let us explain responsibly.
  • No settlement-time promises. Older EPFO pages mention timelines, but they are undated and conflict with newer notices, so this page leaves them out rather than quote a number you might plan around.
  • No per-office remark decoding beyond what official sources support.
  • No advice on whether to withdraw or transfer - that depends on your situation, not on anything a web page can know.

Where to check and where to complain

  • Claim status: checkable on epfindia.gov.in; the paper Form 19's instructions also note SMS alerts at approval stages when your mobile number is on that form.
  • EPFiGMS: EPFO's own grievance portal. Quote your UAN and the exact rejection remark.
  • CPGRAMS: the central government grievance portal, for when EPFiGMS does not resolve it.
EPFO's own services are free. Be wary of anyone who asks for payment in exchange for guaranteed approval, priority processing or "settling" your claim - no one can guarantee an outcome. The official portals are free to use yourself.
